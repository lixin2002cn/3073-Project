02 / THE SETTING

A market has
its own
complications.

Financial data brings structure, uncertainty, and changing conditions. Their importance varies across assets and time horizons.

↳ Where assumptions
meet reality.
Financial background ↗
01The relationships can changeNON-STATIONARITY+

Policies, participants, and economic conditions evolve. A relationship that appears useful in one period may weaken or reverse in another. Adaptation and stability can pull in different directions.

02Observations are connectedDEPENDENCE & CORRELATION+

Many stocks respond to the same event. Neighbouring observations may also share information. A large number of records does not automatically provide an equally large number of independent observations.

03The path matters, tooTAIL RISK & DRAWDOWN+

Average return leaves out part of the story. A few extreme days can substantially change a result, and recovering from a loss can take time. Two portfolios with similar final wealth may have very different journeys.

04Accuracy and returns differPREDICTION & PAYOFF+

The size of price moves, position sizes, and holding periods all affect returns. A model can predict many small moves correctly and still lose money on a few large mistakes.

05Actions have a costTURNOVER & EXECUTION+

Changing a portfolio involves trading. Fees, bid–ask spreads, and slippage can reduce a paper advantage. A simulation captures only the execution effects included in its rules.

Understanding fees · Investor.gov ↗
06History can be misleadingSELECTION & INFORMATION+

A strategy selected from many alternatives may fit one historical period unusually well. Future observations, revised data, and a language model's memory of historical events can also make a result look more predictive than it is.

About this illustration ↗

The three experts represent virtual strategy funds. Their 60-day returns are generated by fixed functions. The adaptive portfolio uses the weights available at the start of each day, then applies wᵢ ← wᵢ exp(η rᵢ / 0.01) and normalizes for the next day. The equal-weight portfolio returns to one third per expert each day.

Each day reveals its returns and a fictional news brief together. The news shows another type of market data: text. These demo experts do not read the news; their returns are fixed. A headline alone does not explain a day's price move.

Both portfolios pay the same one-way fee on traded notional, including the initial investment. Trades account for weight drift and the cash needed to pay fees. Internal trading within the virtual funds, slippage, and market impact are omitted. Maximum drawdown is measured from the highest observed portfolio value.

This illustration uses synthetic teaching data, not real market returns. The illustration does not establish a regret guarantee or imply profitability in financial markets.
